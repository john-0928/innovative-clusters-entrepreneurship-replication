## Description

Urban entrepreneurial activity is highly uneven across space, making place-based industrial policy central to sustainable urban development. This study exploits the phased rollout of China's innovative industrial cluster policy as a quasi-natural experiment and uses new-firm registration data for 284 cities from 2009 to 2024. The manuscript reports that the policy increased urban entrepreneurial vitality in pilot cities relative to non-pilot cities through resource agglomeration, easing financing constraints and expanding market demand, and generated spatial spillovers that peaked near 100 km and continued to about 200 km.

This first replication record contains processed city-level panel data, Stata empirical-analysis code, spatial-weight inputs and matrices, annual Moran's I results and reported result tables. Figure-source data and full figure-generation code will be deposited in a separate linked DOI record. The original firm-level registration data are not included because the manuscript reports database-access and commercial-licensing restrictions.
